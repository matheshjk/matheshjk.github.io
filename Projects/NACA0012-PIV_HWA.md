# Experimental Investigation of NACA0012 Wake Using PIV and Hot-Wire Anemometry

## Overview
This project investigates the wake characteristics of a **NACA0012 airfoil** using two experimental flow diagnostic techniques:

- **Particle Image Velocimetry (PIV)**
- **Hot-Wire Anemometry (HWA)**

The objective was to study the **wake velocity field and turbulence characteristics** downstream of the airfoil and to compare measurements obtained from the two techniques.

While PIV provides spatially resolved velocity fields across a plane in the wake, HWA allows high-frequency measurements of velocity fluctuations at a point. Combining both methods enables detailed analysis of **mean flow behaviour and turbulent structures** in the airfoil wake.

---

# Experimental Setup

The experiments were conducted in the **W tunnel at TU Delft** using a NACA0012 airfoil mounted in the test section.

![](/images/FMT_setup.png)

Two measurement techniques were used:

**Particle Image Velocimetry (PIV)**  
A laser sheet illuminated seeded flow particles in the wake of the airfoil. Consecutive images captured by a camera were processed to compute the velocity field.

**Hot-Wire Anemometry (HWA)**  
A hot-wire probe was positioned in the wake to measure high-frequency velocity fluctuations, enabling analysis of turbulence intensity and spectral characteristics.

---

# Measurement Techniques

## Particle Image Velocimetry (PIV)

PIV was used to obtain **planar velocity measurements** in the wake region downstream of the airfoil.

The method works by:

1. Seeding the flow with tracer particles
2. Illuminating the measurement plane with a laser sheet
3. Capturing successive particle images
4. Using cross-correlation algorithms to compute velocity vectors

This technique provides a **two-dimensional velocity field**, allowing visualization of wake structures and velocity deficits.

---

## Hot-Wire Anemometry (HWA)

Hot-wire anemometry measures flow velocity based on the cooling effect of the fluid on a heated wire.

The voltage signal from the wire is related to the local flow velocity, allowing measurement of:

- instantaneous velocity
- velocity fluctuations
- turbulence intensity

The high temporal resolution of HWA makes it suitable for analysing **turbulent wake dynamics**.

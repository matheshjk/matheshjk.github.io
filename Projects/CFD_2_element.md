# CFD Analysis of a Two-Element Airfoil

## Overview
This project investigates the aerodynamic performance of a **two-element high-lift airfoil configuration** using Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD). The objective was to analyse how the interaction between the **main element and the flap** affects the flow field, pressure distribution and aerodynamic forces.

Two-element airfoils are commonly used in high-lift systems for aircraft during **take-off and landing**, where increased lift is required at relatively low flight speeds. The CFD simulations allow detailed examination of the complex flow structures generated by the slot between the elements.

The study evaluates:

- pressure distribution on both airfoil elements  
- velocity and streamline patterns in the slot region  
- aerodynamic lift generation mechanisms

---

# Numerical Method

## CFD Setup
The flow was simulated using a **Reynolds-Averaged Navier–Stokes (RANS)** approach to capture the viscous flow behaviour around the airfoil.

The computational workflow consisted of:

1. **Geometry preparation**

The two-element airfoil configuration consists of a main airfoil (NLR7301) and a downstream flap separated by a slot.

2. **Mesh generation**

A structured mesh was generated around the airfoil geometry with increased refinement near the airfoil surfaces and in the slot region to accurately resolve boundary layer and shear layer behaviour.

3. **Boundary conditions**
The operational conditions are:

- Freestream Mach No - 0.185
- Chord Length - 0.596m
- Static Temperature - 293K
- Static Pressure - 101325Pa

Typical aerodynamic boundary conditions were applied:

- velocity inlet  
- pressure outlet  
- no-slip condition on airfoil surfaces

4. **Mesh generation**

A 2d geometry of the computational domain around the two element airfoil was created using SOILDWORKS with guidelines for mesh block edges. These edges were constructured in such a way to maximise the orthogonal quality of the mesh. A structured mesh was generated in ICEM with one C grid for each element. This arrangement gave the best quality mesh in the slot gap between the elements. A Mesh independence study was carried out before arriving at the final mesh with 200962 elements with a mean orthogonal quality of 0.964 and mean skew of 0.918 and y+ <1.

5. **Flow solution**

The RANS equations were solved iteratively until the aerodynamic coefficients converged.

# Flow Physics

The presence of the slot between the main element and the flap significantly alters the flow field.

The high-velocity jet through the slot energizes the boundary layer over the flap, delaying separation and increasing lift. This interaction between the elements allows the configuration to achieve higher lift coefficients compared to a single airfoil.

CFD simulations make it possible to visualise:

- acceleration of flow through the slot  
- pressure recovery over the flap  
- wake development downstream of the airfoil system

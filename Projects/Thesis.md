# Experimental Investigation of Boattail Radius Effects for Hammerhead Launcher Flows

## Overview
This thesis investigates how **boattail radius** influences the transonic flow field around a **hammerhead fairing (HHF)** configuration. The work focuses on the Coe and Nute Model 11 and examines how geometric changes at the boattail modify shock structure, separation behaviour, and the unsteady dynamics of the wake.

The study was motivated by the lack of open literature on the aerodynamic effects of **boattail curvature** in hammerhead fairings, despite their relevance to launcher design and buffet loading. The work combines multiple experimental techniques to capture both the mean flow topology and the dominant unsteady structures.

---

## Experimental Approach
The investigation was carried out through two main experimental campaigns involving the following measurement and analysis techniques:

- **High-speed schlieren imaging** to capture shock patterns and their motion across all radius configurations
- **Oil-flow visualisation** to identify separation and reattachment patterns on the surface
- **Particle Image Velocimetry (PIV)** to obtain velocity fields in selected cases
- **POD and SPOD analysis** to identify dominant coherent structures in the separated flow

The boattail radii were selected using an estimate of the turbulent boundary-layer thickness at separation, allowing the radius-to-boundary-layer ratio to vary from order one to much larger values.

---

## Key Findings
Introducing a boattail radius created an additional **expansion region** over the curved section, in several cases followed by a **third shock**. The configurations could be grouped into weak and strong radius-effect cases depending on how prominent this additional shock system became.

Compared with the baseline sharp boattail, the rounded cases showed:

- a **reduction in reattachment length**
- altered shock–shear-layer interaction
- stronger oscillation of the reattachment point in the larger-radius cases

The modal analysis showed two dominant unsteady motions in the separated region:

- a **flapping mode**, associated with reattachment-point motion
- an **undulation mode**, associated with momentum exchange within the separation bubble

These results show that increasing boattail radius can shorten the separated region, but it also introduces additional shock structures that could intensify the unsteady behaviour of the flow.

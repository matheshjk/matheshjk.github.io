# OEI Propeller Aircraft – Experimental & Acoustic Analysis

## Overview

This project investigates the aerodynamic and acoustic effects of **One Engine Inoperative (OEI)** conditions on a twin-propeller aircraft configuration using wind tunnel experiments and statistical modelling.

The study focuses on quantifying:

- Yawing moment asymmetry due to thrust imbalance  
- Wake interaction effects between propellers  
- Blade Passing Frequency (BPF) noise imbalance  
- Sensitivity of aerodynamic and acoustic responses to key design parameters  

---

## Problem Statement

In OEI conditions, asymmetric thrust leads to:

- Significant yawing moments  
- Altered wake structure  
- Increased unsteady loading  
- Noise imbalance between propellers  

Understanding these effects is critical for:

- Aircraft controllability  
- Stability margins  
- Noise certification  
- Early-stage aircraft design  

---

## Methodology

### Experimental Setup

Wind tunnel testing was performed on a twin-propeller aircraft configuration under controlled OEI conditions.

Key measurements included:

- Aerodynamic forces and moments  
- Acoustic pressure signals  
- Propeller rotational effects  

---

### Design of Experiments (DoE)

A **5-factor, second-order Response Surface Methodology (RSM)** test matrix was developed.

This allowed systematic variation of:

- Propeller operating conditions  
- Flow parameters  
- Configuration variables  

The approach enabled efficient mapping of system behaviour with a limited number of experiments.

---

### Data Processing

- Time-resolved signals were analysed to extract **Blade Passing Frequency (BPF)** components  
- Aerodynamic coefficients were computed from balance measurements  
- Statistical models were fitted to identify dominant parameter effects  

---

## Results

### Aerodynamic Effects

- OEI conditions resulted in significant **yaw asymmetry**
- Strong dependence of yawing moment on propeller operating parameters  
- Interaction effects between operating and non-operating propellers influenced the wake structure  

---

### Acoustic Effects

- Observed **~5 dB imbalance in Blade Passing Frequency (BPF) noise** between operating and inactive propellers :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}  
- Increased tonal noise levels under asymmetric operating conditions  
- Clear coupling between aerodynamic loading and acoustic response  

---

### Statistical Modelling Insights

- Response Surface Model successfully captured nonlinear interactions  
- Identified dominant parameters influencing both aerodynamic and acoustic outputs  
- Enabled prediction of system behaviour across the design space  

---

## Key Takeaways

• OEI conditions introduce strong aerodynamic asymmetry affecting stability  
• Wake–propeller interactions play a significant role in system behaviour  
• Acoustic imbalance (~5 dB BPF difference) is a critical design consideration  
• RSM-based DoE is highly effective for multi-parameter experimental studies
